# Scoping and budget lens

Covers making a design buildable: the order of work, missing team skills, rough time and budget ranges, prioritization and cuts.

## When to use

- A beginner or small team has an idea and asks what to build first.
- The team wonders whether it is missing skills or people.
- Someone needs a rough time or budget range, or a deadline sanity check.
- There are more features than time, and something must be cut or postponed.

## Gather

Ask only for what changes the estimate:

- Scope: genre, content volume (levels, characters, hours of play), systems, online features, platforms.
- Target milestone: prototype, vertical slice, demo, early access, full release.
- Team: people, disciplines, experience, hours per week, and whether they are paid.
- Location or cost region, and the work model (salaried, contractors, revenue share).
- Hard constraints: a deadline, funding available, required platforms.

If the team is unknown, estimate for a minimal team and say so.

## Checks

### Order of work

1. Is the plan to prove the core loop first, then build a vertical slice, then produce content? Producing content before the loop works is the most expensive mistake.
2. Which systems can wait until the core is proven: meta progression, monetization, multiplayer, localization, a level editor? Postpone them unless the fantasy depends on them.
3. What is the first milestone, and what does "done" look like for it?

### Scope drivers

1. Name the biggest cost drivers: content volume, the number of interacting systems, online and multiplayer, multiple platforms, voiced narrative, live operations, a high-fidelity art style, procedural generation that must be tuned.
2. For each driver, is there a cheaper version that keeps the fantasy?

### Team coverage

1. For the target milestone, list needed disciplines: design, programming, art, animation, audio, UI and UX, writing, QA, production, marketing and community, business and legal.
2. Mark each as covered, weak or missing. Distinguish gaps that block the milestone from gaps that only matter later.
3. For each gap, choose: learn it, hire, contract, use bought assets or tools, or cut the scope that needs it.

### Time range

1. Estimate with three points: optimistic, likely and pessimistic. Base them on people, hours per week and scope, not on enthusiasm.
2. Add time that beginners forget: integration, bug fixing, polish, platform submission and certification, store setup, marketing beats.
3. For a deadline check, compare the deadline with the likely estimate and say what must be cut to fit.

### Budget range

1. People multiplied by months dominates most budgets; estimate it first, adjusted for region and work model.
2. Add other buckets: outsourced art or audio, software and engine fees, hardware and devkits, QA and localization, legal and company costs, platform fees, marketing, and a contingency of about 15 to 30 percent.
3. Give low and high ranges with assumptions. Never present them as quotes.

### Prioritization and cuts

1. Score each candidate feature on impact on the core promise, cost, risk, dependencies and timing (now, later, never).
2. Name the one feature that, if removed, would most simplify the project while hurting the fantasy least.
3. Keep a written cut list so removed ideas do not quietly return.

## Common problems

- **Content before core**: levels and art produced around an unproven loop.
- **Hidden disciplines**: nobody owns audio, QA or marketing until the end.
- **Single-number estimate**: one optimistic date with no range.
- **Forgotten tail**: polish, certification and launch work left out.
- **Scope creep by yes**: every idea accepted, nothing cut.
- **Region-blind budget**: costs copied from a different market or work model.

## Fix menu

- Write a three-milestone plan: prove the loop, vertical slice, production.
- Cut or postpone the biggest scope driver that the fantasy does not need.
- Fill one blocking gap with a contractor or bought assets rather than a new hire.
- Replace the single date with a three-point range and a list of assumptions.
- Add a contingency line and a launch-tail line to the budget.
- Start a cut list and review it at each milestone.

## Reporting notes

- Present estimates as a table: item, low, likely, high, assumptions.
- State the assumptions before the numbers.
- Say plainly when the scope does not fit the team, time or budget, and what would make it fit.
