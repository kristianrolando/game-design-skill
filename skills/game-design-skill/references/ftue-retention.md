# FTUE and retention lens

Covers the first-time user experience, the shape of sessions and goals, the reasons players return, and whether those reasons are healthy.

## When to use

- Players drop in the tutorial or the first session.
- Onboarding feels like a list of instructions rather than play.
- Sessions end without a reason to come back, or never reach a comfortable stopping point.
- Return mechanics (dailies, streaks, events) are being designed or questioned.
- The team needs to know which metrics would reveal these risks.

## Gather

- A step-by-step script or capture of the first 10 to 30 minutes, including anything before play: downloads, accounts, consent dialogs, cutscenes.
- The goal structure: what the player is working toward in the next minute, the next session and the next week.
- Return systems: energy, timers, daily rewards, streaks, events, notifications.
- Funnel or retention data, if any. Do not supply benchmarks the user did not provide.

## Checks

### First-time experience

1. **Time to first meaningful action**: how many seconds or screens before the player does something that matters? List each pre-play blocker.
2. **Hook before explanation**: does the game show why the world or challenge is worth caring about before teaching systems?
3. **Teach by doing**: is each concept introduced through play, one at a time, with a chance to use it right away? Count text boxes in a row.
4. **The guide**: if there is a mentor, narrator or guide, does it motivate and give useful tools, or only issue orders?
5. **Threshold moment**: when does the player first act on their own and succeed in a way that feels earned? A story-arc framing (call, guide, crossing into the world) helps check that the first session has a shape.
6. **First real choice**: when does the player make a decision that expresses preference?

### Goals and session shape

1. Is there always a visible next goal at three horizons: right now, this session, and longer-term?
2. Are goals immediate and concrete ("reach the bridge") or vague ("get stronger")?
3. Where are the natural stopping points, and does the player feel satisfied stopping there?
4. Does the session end with an open thread that makes the next session appealing?
5. Unfinished tasks pull attention (the Zeigarnik effect). How many are open at once? Too many becomes clutter and guilt.

### Return reasons

1. List each reason to return and classify it: anticipation (something to look forward to), curiosity (an open question), plans (the player's own goals), social (friends, a team), obligation (streaks, decay, expiring offers).
2. Healthy retention leans on the first four. Heavy reliance on obligation and loss is a finding, especially for younger audiences.
3. What happens to a player who returns after two weeks away? Are they punished, lost, or welcomed back with a catch-up?

### Measurement

1. For each risk found, name the metric that would show it: tutorial step funnel, time to first action, first-session length, day-1 and day-7 return, quit points, feature usage.
2. Check whether the build or plan can capture those events. Missing coverage is a next step, not a finding about players.

## Common problems

- **Pre-play wall**: account, download or exposition before any play.
- **Instruction dump**: several mechanics taught in a row with no use between them.
- **Passenger onboarding**: the player follows arrows and never chooses.
- **Goal fog**: no visible next objective after the tutorial ends.
- **No safe stop**: sessions end mid-task or only through a loss.
- **Obligation retention**: streaks, decay and expiring rewards as the main pull.
- **Punished return**: a lapsed player faces lost progress and a cluttered inbox.

## Fix menu

- Move one meaningful action before the first blocker; defer accounts and long downloads.
- Split teaching into "learn one thing, use it, then learn the next".
- Give the player one free choice in the first five minutes.
- Show a next goal on the results screen of every session.
- Replace one obligation mechanic with an anticipation or curiosity hook.
- Add a returning-player path: a recap, a catch-up reward, a cleaned-up inbox.
- Add analytics events for each tutorial step and the first-session quit point.

## Reporting notes

- Report the FTUE as a short timeline with drop risks marked at specific steps.
- Classify each return mechanic as healthy or pressure-based and say why.
- Label drop-off predictions as Inferred unless funnel data exists.
