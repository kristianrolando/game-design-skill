# Multiplayer and social lens

Covers how players relate to each other: structure of play together, the social features players expect, prosocial design, status systems, safety, population health and social monetization.

## When to use

- Designing or reviewing co-op, competitive, team or massively shared play.
- Social features exist but players stay solo.
- Communities turn toxic, or new players get stomped.
- Leaderboards, ranks, clans or a premium pass are under discussion.
- The game depends on enough players being online.

## Gather

- Mode list: solo, co-op, competitive, team versus team, shared world; synchronous or asynchronous; group sizes.
- Social features: parties, friends, chat, pings, emotes, guilds, trading, gifting, spectating.
- Ranking, matchmaking and leaderboard rules.
- Moderation and reporting tools.
- Expected player counts by region and platform, if known.
- Monetization touching other players: cosmetics, passes, boosts.

## Checks

### Social structure

1. Classify each mode: cooperate, compete, or cooperate in order to compete (a team against a team, a guild in a season).
2. Do players actually need each other? Real interdependence (roles, combos, shared resources) builds bonds; parallel solo play next to strangers rarely does.
3. Synchronous play gives intensity but needs schedules; asynchronous play fits busy players but needs a visible trace of others. Does the choice match the audience?

### Social feature tiers

Sort social features by how they affect satisfaction.

1. **Foundations**: their absence causes anger, while their presence goes unnoticed. Examples: inviting friends, fair matchmaking, block and report, leaver handling, communication without typing (pings).
2. **Delighters**: their presence creates warmth, their absence is rarely noticed. Examples: shared celebrations, emotes, gifting, highlights.
3. **High-risk features**: big upside and big downside. Examples: open voice and text chat, trading, full-loot PvP, griefable building.
4. Missing foundations come before any delighter. List high-risk features with their safeguards.

### Prosocial design

1. **Before play**: do role choice, shared goals and framing set up cooperation?
2. **During play**: are there small, frequent helping moments, such as revives, assists, resource sharing and shared objectives?
3. **After play**: do commendations, shared rewards, replay highlights and easy re-grouping carry goodwill forward?
4. Does anything reward hurting teammates, such as kill-stealing, loot races or blame for team losses?

### Status and leaderboards

1. Can a typical player see a rank they can realistically improve, through friend, regional or tiered boards, instead of only a global top 100?
2. Do seasons or resets refresh the competition without erasing identity?
3. Are boards exposed to cheating or to smurfing? Are new players shielded from veterans?
4. Do vanity items signal effort, skill or spending, and is that visible to others?

### Safety and population

1. List griefing and harassment vectors and the tool that answers each one.
2. Check age-appropriate defaults for chat and contact if minors may play.
3. At low player counts, does matchmaking still find a fair match in acceptable time? Is there a fallback: bots, cross-play, asynchronous modes or solo content?

### Social monetization

1. Does any purchase change who wins? Even perceived pay-to-win damages competitive trust.
2. For premium or battle passes: is track pacing achievable for the target player's real schedule, is the value clear, and does missing out feel acceptable rather than punishing?

## Common problems

- **Lonely multiplayer**: players share a space but never need each other.
- **Missing foundations**: no block, report, ping or leaver handling, while new delighters ship.
- **Global-only ladder**: most players see a rank they can never move.
- **Newcomer slaughter**: no protection from veterans or smurfs.
- **Empty lobby risk**: the game assumes more concurrent players than it will have.
- **Pay-to-win perception**: purchases that affect outcomes, or appear to.

## Fix menu

- Add one interdependence mechanic, such as a role combo, revive or shared objective.
- Ship missing foundations before new social extras.
- Add friend and tiered leaderboards with seasonal resets.
- Add new-player matchmaking protection and smurf detection rules.
- Plan a low-population fallback before launch.
- Keep paid items cosmetic in competitive modes, and tune pass length to a realistic play schedule.

## Reporting notes

- Present social features as a table: feature, tier, status (present or missing), risk.
- Separate design findings from operational ones (moderation staffing, servers) and name the owner for each.
- Population assumptions are Assumed unless the user provided numbers.
